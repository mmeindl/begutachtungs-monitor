# What could the monitor do harm to?

**As of 2026-11-23.** Research memo answering one question: what are the risks
and side effects, for democracy, society and people's wellbeing, of making
lawmaking more transparent and taking part in it easier?

This is reasoned judgement. Nobody has measured the monitor's effects on
anyone, and the site counts no visitors, so it cannot show who uses it. The
studies below come from other countries and settings. They say what to watch
for, not what will happen here. Their citations were checked on 2026-11-23;
the papers were not read in full for this memo.

## Findings in four sentences

1. **The monitor is most likely net positive,** because it only reads and
   shows public procedure and decides nothing itself.
2. **The largest risks come from how others react:** decisions moving out of
   sight, organised interests gaining more than citizens, and people who
   expected a vote feeling ignored.
3. **Most risks grow with the monitor's success,** so they are cheaper to
   answer now than after it is widely used.
4. **The rules already cover much of this** (below); the open gaps are equal
   access, mass-produced input, the order of the statements and the Gemeinden.

## The three largest risks

### 1. Decisions move to where the monitor cannot see them

Transparency about a decision can change how it is made. Prat shows that
observing an agent's actions, rather than their consequences, can make the
agent conform instead of using what they know. Malesky, Schuler and Tran
published the records of randomly chosen delegates in Vietnam's National
Assembly and found no improvement in their work. That is an authoritarian
assembly without electoral sanction, and a published comment disputes the
conclusions, so it is a warning and not a forecast for Austria.

Here the same pressure could mean bargaining earlier and informally, thinner
Erläuterungen, or skipping the Begutachtung. The route exists regardless of
the monitor: 57 of 205 federal laws of the current period had no Begutachtung,
and 42 of 140 expired drafts without a Regierungsvorlage became law as an
Initiativantrag (GP XXVI–XXVIII, text overlap ≥ 0.6; `decisions.md`, section 2).

**Answer:** keep listing the laws without a Begutachtung. Their share per
period is the early warning. It cannot prove that the monitor caused a change.

### 2. It may help the already powerful most

Political voice is unequal by education, income and organisation, organised
interests are less representative than individuals, and online participation
repeats the pattern (Schlozman, Verba and Brady). A free comparison of every
draft is worth most to whoever already has staff to read it: a chamber, an
association, a law firm, a lobbyist. More voices can then mean a wider gap.

**Answer:** design for someone with little time and no legal training, and
ask partner organisations who actually uses the tool. Not built yet.

### 3. Visible participation can produce disillusion

A Begutachtung is input, not a vote. Nobody is obliged to read, answer or
explain (`stellungnahme-was-danach-passiert.md`). Someone who expected a vote
and sees a law pass unchanged may conclude that taking part is pointless. A
review of 187 studies finds that transparency tends to raise participation and
is less effective at building trust and legitimacy (Cucciniello, Porumbescu
and Grimmelikhuijsen). Lessig warns that showing links without showing cause
can inspire „not reform, but disgust".

**Answer, since 23.11.2026:** the door card says what a reader can expect
after filing (`StatementExpectation`; `decisions.md`, „What a reader can expect
from a Stellungnahme").

## Other side effects

| Risk | What could happen | State |
|---|---|---|
| Mass and fake input | Of more than 22 million comments in the FCC's 2017 net neutrality proceeding, nearly 18 million were fake, from both sides (New York Attorney General, 2021). Generative AI makes this cheaper. | Open. The monitor offers no template or one-click filing and should not. |
| Counts read as votes | A campaign's numbers look like a public verdict on a law. | Reduced since 23.11.2026: statements open newest first, „Meiste Zustimmungen" is one click away. |
| Numbers out of context | A screenshot of „57 laws without Begutachtung" feeds an „all corrupt" story. | Reduced by the framing rules; a screenshot escapes them. |
| Pressure on people | Civil servants and small organisations become targets and grow more cautious. | Private persons are never named. Small named organisations remain exposed. |
| Privacy at the Gemeinde level | A zoning notice can identify a neighbour, a plot or a home address. Search and aggregation change what a public notice means. | Open. Needs its own rule before any municipal data is read (`gemeinderecht.md`). |
| Open data backfires | Pressure produces formal compliance: poor PDFs, fewer informative documents, or „the monitor already does this". | Open. Worth raising whenever asking a public body to publish more. |
| Trust concentrates in one tool | One parsing error spreads widely, what is shown becomes quiet editorial power, and the tool's end leaves a gap. | Reduced by stated gaps, published accuracy, open source and low running costs. |
| Attention and wellbeing | Constant deadlines add to outrage and fatigue, above all for small NGOs and volunteers. | Reduced: no alerts, no urgency beyond the real Frist. |

## What the existing rules already do

No blame counters or accusation badges, no figure presented as the effect of a
Stellungnahme, no ranking by outcome, private persons counted and never named,
proof before any claim of absence, every gap named, and no tracking
(`CLAUDE.md`; `decisions.md`, „Framing and what the page may claim").

## What follows

1. **Done:** say what a Stellungnahme can do, where people file it.
2. **Keep as a rule:** no one-click or template submission. Making
   participation easier means making it easier to understand, not cheaper to
   mass-produce.
3. **Open:** a route for the reader with twenty minutes and no legal training.
4. **Open:** a privacy rule for the Gemeinden before any of their data is read.
5. **Done:** statements open newest first, not by „Meiste Zustimmungen",
   which put the best-mobilised on top.
